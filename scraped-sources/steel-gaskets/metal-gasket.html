1. Spiral Wound Gaskets

High-performance semi-metallic gaskets comprising a V-shaped metal wound strip and soft fillers such as graphite or PTFE, exhibiting higher recovery when there is a variation in conditions.

 

  • Advantages: Great resilience, thermal stability, and chemical resistance.
  • Applications: Heat exchangers, flanged pipe joints, and the chemical and petrochemical plants.
  • Standards: ASME B16.20, API 601.

2. Ring Type Joint (RTJ) Gaskets

Extreme high-pressure gasket. Solid metallic gaskets that can be used in high-temperature gasket applications, which offer a safe metal-to-metal seal.

  • Advantages: Sealing with the highest levels of reliability, resistance to vibration, and high service life.
  • Applications: Oil and gas pipelines and wellheads, high-pressure valves, pressure vessels.
  • Standards: API 6A, ASME B16.20.

3. Kammprofile Gaskets

Serrated metal core industrial gaskets and soft facing layers provide the benefit of providing strength and effective sealing across a range of loads.

  • Advantages: Reduced bolting load, superior sealing, and redesignable.
  • Applications: Boilers, manways, narrow flanged heat exchangers.
  • Standards: ASME B16.20, DIN EN 1514-6.

4. Corrugated Metal Gaskets (CMG)

Metal gaskets with inexpensive cores, corrugated and lined with soft seals, are perfect in strenuous flange applications.

  • Advantages: Stable seal on irregular surfaces, compressible, pressure-recovery.
  • Applications: Compressors, exhaust, heat exchangers.
  • Standards: DIN EN 1514-4, ASME B16.20.

5. Metal Jacketed Gaskets

A soft filler is surrounded by a metallic jacket (single or twice) that unites the chemical resistance and compressibility of filler materials with the strength and durability of metal. Appropriate for sealing high-temperature and high-pressure environments.

  • Advantages: Seals at a low cost, resistant to blowout, can withstand extreme environments, and can be customized to suit the diverse industries.
  • Applications: Heat exchangers, valves, pumps, and high-pressure vessels.
  • Standards: ASME B16.20, DIN EN 1514-4.

Metal Gasket HS Code

The Harmonized System (HS) Code is a standardized global system for classifying traded products. Metal gaskets are classified under HS Code 8484, which covers gaskets and similar joints made of metal sheeting, combined with other materials, or composed of two or more metal layers. This classification ensures efficient and accurate trade transactions.

Standards for Metal Gaskets

Metal gaskets adhere to standards set by organizations to ensure uniformity, reliability, and consistent performance. Key standards include:

  • ASME Standards: For example, ASME B16.20 for spiral wound gaskets in flanged connections.
  • API Standards: Such as API 6A for wellhead and Christmas tree equipment, including ring joint gaskets.
  • DIN Standards: For example, DIN 2696 for metal gaskets with flexible graphite fillers.
  • ISO Standards: Including ISO 9001 for quality management systems.
  • ASTM Standards: Such as ASTM F104 for non-metallic flat gaskets.
  • EN Standards: Including EN 1514 for gasket dimensions and tolerances for compatibility with European flange norms.

Answers to all your possible questions.

What is a metal gasket used for?

A metal gasket is a high-performance sealing component made from solid or semi-metallic materials designed to withstand high pressure, high temperature, and corrosive environments. Metal gaskets are commonly used in industrial flange connections in oil & gas, power plants, and chemical processing systems.

What materials are used in metallic gaskets?

Metallic gaskets are manufactured from stainless steel (304, 316), carbon steel, Inconel, Monel, and other special alloys. Depending on the application, they may include graphite or PTFE fillers to improve sealing performance and chemical resistance.

What is the difference between metal and semi-metallic gaskets?

Solid metal gaskets are made entirely of metal and are used in extreme pressure conditions, such as RTJ gaskets. Semi-metallic gaskets, like spiral wound gaskets, combine a metal core with soft filler material to provide flexibility and enhanced sealing performance.

How do metal gaskets improve plant safety?

By providing strong mechanical strength, thermal stability, and corrosion resistance, metal gaskets minimize leakage risks, reduce downtime, and ensure compliance with international standards like ASME, DIN, BS, API, and ISO.

Can metal gaskets be customized?

Yes, metal gaskets can be manufactured in standard sizes or custom-engineered configurations to meet specific flange designs and operational requirements.

Which metal gasket is best for high-pressure applications?

For extreme pressure applications, Ring Type Joint (RTJ) gaskets are commonly recommended. For fluctuating pressure and temperature conditions, spiral wound metal gaskets offer excellent sealing reliability.

What standards do your metal gaskets comply with?

Our metal gaskets are manufactured according to ASME, API, DIN, BS, and ISO standards, ensuring dimensional accuracy, pressure compatibility, and long service life in industrial flange applications.

How do I select the right metal gasket?

Selecting the correct metal gasket depends on operating pressure, temperature, media compatibility, flange type, and surface finish. Our technical team assists in choosing the most suitable industrial gasket for your specific application.

Are metal gaskets better than rubber gaskets?

Yes, metal gaskets and semi-metallic gaskets perform significantly better than rubber gaskets in high-pressure, high-temperature, and chemically aggressive environments. Metallic gaskets provide superior mechanical strength, thermal stability, and resistance to corrosion, making them ideal for industrial flange applications where rubber gaskets may fail or degrade.

Do metal gaskets require sealant?

In most industrial applications, metal gaskets do not require additional sealant when properly selected and installed according to flange specifications and torque requirements. However, in certain low-pressure or surface-imperfection cases, a compatible gasket sealant may be used to enhance sealing performance.
