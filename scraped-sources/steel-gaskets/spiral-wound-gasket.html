What is a Spiral Wound Gasket (SPW)?

A Spiral Wound Gasket (SPW) is a high-performance semi-metallic sealing component engineered for industrial piping systems where a standard gasket would fail. It is the primary choice for high-pressure, high-temperature, and corrosive environments, providing a robust seal that adapts to fluctuating operating conditions.

The unique performance of an SWG comes from its composite construction:

  • Metallic Winding: A pre-formed V-shaped metal strip (commonly Stainless Steel 316L or specialty alloys) provides structural strength and mechanical resilience.
  • Soft Filler Material: Alternating layers of soft materials such as Flexible Graphite, PTFE, or Mica are wound with the metal to fill flange irregularities and ensure a gas-tight seal.

This specialized “V-shape” design allows the gasket to act like a heavy-duty spring, creating a dynamic seal with high elastic recovery. This ensures that even if the flange moves due to thermal expansion or vibration, the spiral wound gasket maintains constant tension against the flange faces to prevent leakage.

Type GI (Gasket with Inner Ring)

The gasket is made solely of the V-shaped metal winding and a non-metallic filler material (such as graphite or PTFE).

  • Consists only of the spiral wound sealing element (no inner or outer rings)
  • Relies on the compression of the spiral windings to fill flange face irregularities.
  • Used exclusively with flanges that provide their own centering mechanism.
  • Ideal for tongue and groove or male-to-female flanges in vessels, valves, and pumps.
  • Not suitable for raised-face or flat-face flanges due to the lack of a centering or compression stop ring.

Installation of Spiral Wound Metallic Gaskets

Flange Preparation

  • Remove the old gasket and thoroughly clean flange faces to eliminate debris, corrosion, or residue.
  • Ensure flange surfaces are free from radial (cross-face) scoring, as this can cause leakage.
  • A surface finish of 3.2 µm to 6.3 µm Ra (125–250 micro-inches) is recommended for spiral wound gaskets.
  • Check that flange faces are parallel and concentric, or that pipework flexibility allows proper alignment without excessive bolt loading.

Gasket Handling

  • Always use a new spiral wound gasket for each installation.
  • Verify that gasket dimensions, pressure class, and type match the flange specification.
  • Do not apply jointing compounds, grease, or lubricants to metallic gaskets.
  • If temporary fixing is required, use only a light spray adhesive to position the gasket.

Installation Procedure

  • Position the gasket centrally between the flange faces.
  • Insert bolts and finger-tighten nuts evenly.
  • Tighten bolts using a controlled method such as torque or tension tightening, following a star or cross-bolt sequence.
  • Complete final tightening to the specified torque, ensuring uniform gasket compression.
  • After installation, confirm that flange faces remain parallel.

Answers to all your possible questions.

What is a spiral wound gasket used for?

A spiral wound gasket is used to create a leak-proof seal in high-pressure and high-temperature flange joints. It is commonly installed in pipelines, pressure vessels, heat exchangers, and valves where resistance to thermal cycling, vibration, and fluctuating bolt loads is required.

What temperature can spiral wound gaskets withstand?

Spiral wound gaskets can typically withstand temperatures ranging from -200°C to +550°C, depending on the metal winding and filler material used. Flexible graphite fillers offer higher temperature resistance, while PTFE fillers are suited for lower-temperature and chemically aggressive applications.

Why use a Spiral Gasket?

Spiral wound gaskets have established themselves as the most dependable sealing components suitable for challenging, vital, and demanding applications. Spiral wound gaskets offer excellent sealing performance, compatibility with various media, resilience to high temperatures and pressures, and the ability to compensate for flange irregularities, making them a reliable choice for demanding sealing applications.

What materials are used in spiral wound gaskets?

Spiral wound gaskets are manufactured using alternating metal windings and soft filler materials. Common metal windings include stainless steel 304, 316, and carbon steel, while filler materials include flexible graphite, PTFE, ceramic fiber, or vermiculite, selected based on pressure, temperature, and media compatibility.

What does the color coding on spiral wound gaskets indicate?

The color coding on spiral wound gaskets indicates the material combination used in the gasket, including the metal winding and filler material. This standardized color system helps users quickly identify gasket composition, temperature limits, and application suitability in accordance with industry standards such as ASME B16.20.

Why is gasket thickness important in spiral wound gaskets?

Gasket thickness is critical because it affects compressibility, seating stress, and sealing performance. Proper thickness ensures adequate elastic recovery under bolt load while preventing over-compression. Incorrect thickness can lead to leakage, uneven flange loading, or premature gasket failure.

When should CGI spiral wound gaskets be used instead of CG types?

CGI spiral wound gaskets should be used instead of CG types when additional radial strength and improved centering are required. The inner ring in CGI gaskets prevents inward buckling and protects the winding from process media, making them suitable for higher pressure and temperature services.

What information is required when ordering spiral wound gaskets?

When ordering spiral wound gaskets, details such as nominal pipe size (NPS), pressure class, flange standard, gasket type (CG, CGI), metal winding material, filler material, thickness, and operating pressure and temperature should be specified to ensure correct selection for the application.

What is a Vermiculite Spiral Wound Gasket?

Vermiculite spiral wound gasket is a critical service gasket designed for use in applications involving high temperatures and aggressive chemical media. And this style gasket is comprised of a spirally wound sealing element composed of a metal winding strip tape in combination with a vermiculite based filler material.

Service and Application of Vermiculite Spiral Wound Gasket?

Vermiculite spiral wound gaskets are suitable for use in a wide range of sealing applications. They are capable of effecting a high integrity seal in the most demanding environments and are particularly suited to applications involving extremes of thermal or mechanical cycling. Typical applications include superheated steam, strong oxidising media and elevated temperature service.
