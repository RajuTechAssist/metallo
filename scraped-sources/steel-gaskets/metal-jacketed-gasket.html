Metal Jacketed Gasket Materials

To ensure dependable sealing performance, metal jacketed gaskets are manufactured using a composite construction that combines mechanical strength with controlled compressibility. The metal jacket functions as a protective outer shell, delivering structural support while acting as the primary barrier against process media and elevated pressures. Jacket materials are selected based on service conditions and commonly include Stainless Steel, Carbon Steel, and Monel to meet specific chemical and thermal requirements. Enclosed within the jacket, the filler core provides the necessary resilience and recovery to conform to flange surfaces and maintain a secure seal. Filler materials such as flexible graphite, PTFE, or ceramic help accommodate thermal expansion, vibration, and mechanical movement during operation.

Ring gaskets are manufactured using various materials based on the application’s requirements. The material selection depends on factors such as the type of fluids being sealed, operating conditions (including temperature and pressure), and the overall environment. Here are some common materials used for manufacturing ring gaskets:

Soft Iron & Carbon Steel

An economical choice for general non-corrosive steam and oil services, suitable for temperatures up to 500°C.

Stainless Steel (304, 316L, 321)

The industry standard for chemical and food processing, offering high corrosion resistance at temperatures up to 550°C (with 321/347 reaching 800°C).

Copper & Brass

Soft and highly malleable materials ideal for uneven flange faces or air compressors, rated for temperatures between 300°C and 400°C.

Monel 400

Provides exceptional resistance to hydrofluoric acid and marine environments, maintaining integrity up to 500°C.

Inconel (600/625)

Engineered for the most demanding environments, providing superior oxidation resistance in extreme heat up to 1000°C.

Aluminum

A lightweight, corrosion-resistant option often used in specialized gas applications with temperatures up to 300°C.

Flexible Graphite

The most popular choice due to its wide temperature range (-200°C to 550°C) and excellent chemical resistance.

PTFE (Teflon)

Used specifically for high-purity or highly aggressive chemical environments where temperature remains below 260°C.

Ceramic Fiber

Reserved for extremely high-temperature applications (up to 1100°C) where other fillers would degrade.

Non-Asbestos Millboard

A cost-effective, general-purpose filler used for heat insulation and lower-pressure sealing up to 500°C.

Double Jacketed Gaskets

    • Constructed with a soft filler fully enclosed between a metal jacket and internal insert
    • Designed for high temperature and high pressure applications requiring stable sealing
    • Offers improved mechanical strength compared to soft gaskets
    • Provides enhanced resistance to blowout and gasket deformation
    • Jacket material can be selected to suit a wide range of process media and service conditions

Exchanger Gaskets [Double Jacketed Gaskets]

    • One of the most commonly used heat exchanger gasket designs in industrial service
    • Available in a broad range of metallic materials suitable for elevated temperature operation
    • Frequently used on standard flanges where operating conditions are moderate
    • Custom fabrication allows flexibility in size, shape, and configuration, including large diameters and pass partition bars

Double Jacketed Corrugated Gasket

    • An enhanced version of the standard double jacketed gasket with added corrugations
    • Corrugations create a secondary sealing path, improving leak resistance
    • Reduced contact area increases compressibility under limited bolt loads
    • Primary sealing action is maintained along the inner lap for consistent performance

Corrugated Metal Gaskets [CMG]

    • A proven corrugated metal gasket design widely used across industrial applications
    • Suitable for ASME Class 150 and 300 flanges where bolt loading is restricted
    • Corrugated geometry improves recovery during thermal cycling and extends service life
    • Can be used as a direct replacement for spiral wound gaskets in many applications
    • Minimizes inward buckling while maintaining effective sealing at moderate flange stresses

Single Jacketed

    • Manufactured with a soft filler core completely enclosed in a single metal jacket
    • Ideal where space constraints do not allow the use of a double jacketed gasket
    • Commonly used in narrow flange widths and compact equipment designs

Answers to all your possible questions.

What is a metal jacketed gasket?

A metal jacketed gasket is a semi-metallic sealing solution consisting of a soft filler core fully or partially enclosed in a metallic outer shell. The metal jacket provides strength and blowout resistance, while the filler ensures compressibility and recovery for effective flange sealing.

Where are metal jacketed gaskets commonly used?

Metal jacketed gaskets are primarily used in heat exchangers, boilers, pressure vessels, reactor systems, and refinery piping. They are ideal for high-temperature, high-pressure applications where conventional soft gaskets may fail.

What are the types of metal jacketed gaskets?

The main types include: 1. Single jacketed gaskets for moderate pressure services 2. Double jacketed gaskets for higher-pressure and heat exchanger applications 3. Corrugated metal jacketed gaskets for improved flexibility under fluctuating loads

What standards govern metal jacketed gaskets?

Metal jacketed gaskets are typically manufactured in accordance with ASME B16.20, DIN, and API standards , ensuring dimensional accuracy, pressure class compliance, and material traceability.

How do I choose the right metal jacketed gasket material for corrosive media?

Material selection depends on process media, operating temperature, and pressure conditions. Stainless steel and Monel jackets are commonly used for corrosive environments, while filler materials such as flexible graphite or PTFE enhance chemical resistance. Our engineering team supports the selection of metal jacketed gasket materials based on specific service requirements.

Are your metal jacketed gaskets compliant with international standards?

Yes. Asian Sealing manufactures ASME B16.20 metal jacketed gaskets and supplies products compliant with ASME B16.5 and ASME B16.47 flange standards. All gaskets are produced under certified quality systems with full material traceability for global industrial and oil & gas applications.

What temperature and pressure ranges can metal jacketed gaskets withstand?

Performance depends on material selection. With appropriate materials, metal jacketed gaskets can operate in temperatures exceeding 500°C and in high-pressure flange assemblies used in refinery and power generation systems.

How do metal jacketed gaskets differ from spiral wound gaskets?

Metal jacketed gaskets use a solid metal shell with a soft core, offering rigidity and suitability for heat exchanger and pass-partition applications. Spiral wound gaskets use alternating layers of metal winding and filler, making them more flexible and suitable for pipeline flanges.

How long do metal jacketed gaskets last?

Service life depends on operating conditions, but with proper installation and maintenance, they provide long-term sealing performance.

How should metal jacketed gaskets be installed?

Ensure clean flange surfaces, correct alignment, and uniform bolt tightening to prevent leakage.

Can metal jacketed gaskets be customized?

Yes. Metal jacketed gaskets can be custom-manufactured in large diameters, non-standard shapes, and pass-partition designs to match specific flange configurations and operating requirements.
